# Voice

The way Postern writes, codified so it does not drift over 20 years.

## The voice in one sentence

Direct, declarative, dry. The voice of a thoughtful builder writing to another thoughtful person, with no time for performance.

## What the voice IS

The welcome tile is the canonical example. Every sentence in it carries a principle. Read each one with the annotation that follows.

> No inbox here.

Three words. States the thesis without selling it. The reader learns more about Postern from this opening than from any tagline. The principle: lead with the actual claim, not a framing of the claim.

> The home screen is threads you're in the middle of, sent or received in the last 14 days.

A definition, written like a definition. No "we believe" or "we've reimagined." The principle: describe the product the way you would describe a tool to a friend, in the same words you would use in a Slack message.

> Pinned threads stay at the top.

Six words. Plain function. The reader needs this fact to use the product, so the sentence delivers it and stops. The principle: a sentence ends when its job is done.

> Each Space (Cmd-1, 2, 3) is a room with its own theme and signature.

The metaphor is "room," chosen because it carries the right weight without requiring explanation. The keyboard shortcuts sit in parens because they are reference information, not narrative. The principle: pick the one right word and trust it. Surround instructions with parens, not with prose.

> Newsletters and receipts get their own home in a later version. Filtered out for now.

Acknowledges what is missing. Tells the truth about the timeline. The principle: when a feature is not built, say so plainly. Do not soften it with "we're working on" or "stay tuned."

> File issues on GitHub when things break. They will.

Two sentences, the second three words. The voice can be funny, but only when the joke is also true. The principle: humor is a side effect of accuracy, not a goal.

> Archive this when you're done.

A direct instruction to the reader, as if from one person to another. Sign-off is just the initial. The principle: write to one reader, not an audience.

## What the voice IS NOT

A list, because lists are clearer than prose for this.

- No em dashes for parenthetical breaks. Use commas, periods, or parens.
- No correlative constructions like "X, not Y" inside body copy. Section titles can carry that shape, the prose cannot.
- No staccato phrasing. "Simple. Clear. Effective." reads as ad copy. Real sentences carry real ideas.
- No AI vocabulary. The blocked list, in alphabetical order: comprehensive, crucial, delve, foster, intricate, leverage, multifaceted, navigate, pivotal, robust, seamless. If a sentence needs one of these words, the sentence is wrong.
- No "However" or "Moreover" as paragraph openers. Start with the point.
- No "It's worth noting." Either it is worth noting and you are noting it, or it is not.
- No significance inflation. "A testament to," "underscores," "speaks to" are filler. Cut them.
- No triplet adjectives. "Powerful, flexible, and intuitive" is the sound of someone with nothing specific to say.
- No exclamation marks in product copy. The exclamation mark is a signal that the writer does not trust the sentence.
- No emojis. They date the document the moment they ship.

## Voice across surfaces

**README.** Plain English, present tense. The first paragraph describes what Postern is and who it is for. The second describes what is built and what is not. No screenshots above the first paragraph of text. No badge wall above the title.

**Marketing site.** One page. Headline, sub-headline, twenty-second demo video, three rule-breaks, link to the README. The site does not have a testimonials section. The site does not have a pricing section. The voice on the site is the voice in the README, set in larger type.

**Error messages.** Tell the user what happened, why, and what to do next. "Couldn't reach Gmail. Your network may be down, or the OAuth token expired. Reconnect from Settings." No apology. No "Oops." No anthropomorphized error. The error message is information, not theater.

**In-app copy.** Labels, tooltips, confirmation dialogs, empty states. Every string is the shortest accurate version of itself. An empty thread list says "No active threads." It does not say "Looks like you're all caught up, nice work!" The voice respects the user's time and assumes the user can read.

**Changelogs.** Plain bullets. Verb first. "Added Boost loader." "Fixed thread sort on first sync." "Removed the snooze button." If a change is a breaking change, it says so in the bullet, not in a separate banner. Each release ends with a line crediting external contributors by handle.

**PR descriptions.** Two sections, no more. What and why. The what is one paragraph. The why is one paragraph. Screenshots if the change is visual. No checklist of buzzwords. The reviewer does not need to be persuaded that the work matters; they need to understand what changed.

## The wrong-sentence test

Before any copy ships, read each sentence and ask: would Marquis, sitting across a table from a friend, actually say this sentence? Not "would Marquis approve this sentence." Would he say it, out loud, in the same words.

If the answer is no, the sentence is wrong. Rewrite it until the answer is yes.

A faster version of the test: read the sentence aloud. If you hear an AI voice in your head while reading, the sentence is wrong. Trust the ear.
